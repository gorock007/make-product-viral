# The 32 Principles of a Viral Product — Full Reference

By Mark Lu. Read this before grading so each verdict is accurate. For each principle: the rule, why it matters, and what "good" (a 3) vs "bad" (a 0) looks like when you inspect a product.

---

### 1. A viral product does not have a free plan
Free users increase support load, server cost, and pressure to build features paying customers don't want. Less than 3% of free users ever convert.
- **3:** No free plan; the path is buy or leave (a trial that requires a card is fine).
- **0:** Prominent forever-free tier carrying the product.
- **N/A:** Open-source / community tool where free is the deliberate model — note the deviation.

### 2. A viral product has three colors
Every color competes for attention. Black text, white background, one accent for the Buy button.
- **3:** Restrained palette; the accent color is reserved almost exclusively for the primary CTA.
- **0:** Rainbow of competing colors; the Buy button doesn't stand out.

### 3. A viral product uses numbers instead of adjectives
"Fast" is forgettable. "Save 4 hours every week" isn't.
- **3:** Concrete numbers/metrics carry the value claims.
- **0:** Vague adjectives ("fast," "powerful," "seamless") with no numbers.

### 4. A viral product ends with a footer people want to share
97% won't buy but might share. People remember what they see last — finish strong.
- **3:** Memorable closing section / share prompt / strong final line.
- **0:** Generic link-dump footer that trails off.

### 5. A viral product treats the OG image as a YouTube thumbnail
The OG image is often seen more than the site itself. If they don't click, they don't watch.
- **3:** Custom, thumbnail-quality `og:image` + `twitter:card` designed to earn the click.
- **0:** No OG image, default framework placeholder, or a bare logo.

### 6. A viral product reveals one idea per screen
One screen communicates one idea and nothing else — like the Instagram feed.
- **3:** Each section delivers a single clear message.
- **0:** Walls of cluttered sections cramming many ideas at once.

### 7. A viral product has a headline a fifth grader can understand
Complexity kills curiosity. Simple words; your mum should get it.
- **3:** Plain-language headline anyone grasps instantly.
- **0:** Jargon-loaded or abstract headline ("Synergistic AI-native orchestration platform").

### 8. A viral product has a hard paywall
Signups don't pay the bills. Ask for payment before asking for data.
- **3:** Payment required up front; clear, confident paywall.
- **0:** Endless free access with no point at which money is asked for.
- **N/A:** Same caveat as #1 for deliberately free tools.

### 9. A viral product has copy only you could write
If a competitor could copy-paste your landing page, your copy is too generic. Write from experience.
- **3:** Specific, opinionated copy rooted in real experience/insight.
- **0:** Interchangeable boilerplate that would fit any competitor.

### 10. A viral product shows the product before it explains it
A demo communicates more than paragraphs. Show, don't tell.
- **3:** Demo / video / interactive preview / strong screenshot above or near the fold.
- **0:** Only text describing the product; no visual proof of it working.

### 11. A viral product does one thing
The more it does, the less people remember. Be known for one thing.
- **3:** A single, clearly-stated core job.
- **0:** "Does everything" positioning; a Swiss Army knife.

### 12. A viral product uses Popcorn Pricing
Every extra tier is another decision and another reason to leave. Three choices: Good, Better, Best.
- **3:** Exactly ~3 clear tiers.
- **0:** Many tiers / a spreadsheet of options, or confusing pricing.

### 13. A viral product rides a wave
Built around trends, tech, or problems people already discuss — the wave does half the marketing.
- **3:** Clearly tied to a current wave (a hot tech, a trending problem).
- **0:** Disconnected from any momentum people are already paying attention to.

### 14. A viral product steals its best copy from customers
Customers describe your product better than you do. Write like they talk.
- **3:** Copy/testimonials in real customer language.
- **0:** Corporate voice with no trace of how customers actually speak.

### 15. A viral product has a founder people can see and hear
People buy from people. A founder screen recording beats a corporate promo. Show your face.
- **3:** Visible founder — face, voice, personal recording, or named human presence.
- **0:** Faceless corporate entity.

### 16. A viral product makes pricing impossible to miss
Visitors use pricing to understand the product. Put "Pricing" in the header.
- **3:** "Pricing" in the nav/header; price is easy to find.
- **0:** Pricing buried, hidden, or "contact us" only.

### 17. A viral product has a headline people remember the next day
Write five headlines, show friends, ask 24h later which stuck.
- **3:** A sticky, distinctive headline.
- **0:** Forgettable, generic headline.

### 18. A viral product has an emotional headline
People remember feelings, not features. Make them laugh, say wow, or "wtf is this."
- **3:** Headline that triggers a real emotional reaction.
- **0:** Flat, purely functional headline.

### 19. A viral product does something people have never seen before
Nobody shares another clone. Surprise people.
- **3:** A genuinely novel angle, mechanic, or presentation.
- **0:** An obvious clone of an existing product.

### 20. A viral product can be sold from the hero section alone
80% won't scroll past the hero. They must understand and want it within seconds. Fix the hero first.
- **3:** Hero alone communicates what it is, who it's for, why it matters, and the next step.
- **0:** Hero leaves visitors confused about what the product even is.
- **This is the single highest-leverage principle — weight your verdict accordingly.**

### 21. A viral product shows empathy before it sells
People must believe you understand their problem before they trust your solution. Describe the problem better than they can.
- **3:** Names the visitor's pain sharply before pitching.
- **0:** Jumps straight to features with no problem framing.

### 22. A viral product has one call to action
Every extra button creates hesitation; multiple paths lead many to choose none. One next step.
- **3:** One dominant, repeated CTA.
- **0:** Many competing CTAs of equal weight.

### 23. A viral product has a name people remember
Use known words. Avoid wordplay, made-up words, names needing explanation.
- **3:** Simple, memorable, easy-to-spell name.
- **0:** Cryptic, hard-to-spell, or explanation-requiring name.

### 24. A viral product sells a human desire, not a feature
People buy more money, more time, better health, more status, or less pain. Features are vehicles.
- **3:** Leads with the outcome/desire.
- **0:** Leads with feature lists and specs.

### 25. A viral product lets people try the product before buying it
Don't hide your best features behind a paywall. Let people play before they pay.
- **3:** Interactive demo / sandbox / sample output on the landing page.
- **0:** Everything locked behind signup or payment.

### 26. A viral product does not use weak words
"most," "many," "rarely" mean nothing concrete. Make statements, not estimates.
- **3:** Clear, falsifiable, confident claims.
- **0:** Hedged, vague qualifiers throughout.

### 27. A viral product does not have a subscription
People have enough subscriptions. One-time payments are 10x easier to sell.
- **3:** One-time payment.
- **0:** Subscription with no strong reason it must be recurring.
- **N/A:** Genuinely consumption/ongoing-cost product (e.g. hosting) — note it.

### 28. A viral product has a call to action that says what happens next
"Get Started" means nothing. "Analyze My Website" tells people exactly what they'll do.
- **3:** Specific, action-revealing CTA label.
- **0:** Generic "Get Started" / "Sign Up" / "Learn More."

### 29. A viral product does not launch without testimonials
A page without testimonials asks strangers to trust you blindly. Collect proof before traffic.
- **3:** Real testimonials / reviews / named social proof.
- **0:** No social proof at all.

### 30. A viral product can be described in under 10 words
If you can't explain it in one sentence, users won't either.
- **3:** A crisp sub-10-word description exists or is obvious from the hero.
- **0:** Takes a paragraph to explain what it is.

### 31. A viral product compares itself to competitors
People care why they should switch. Show a comparison table that makes the decision obvious.
- **3:** Clear comparison table vs. alternatives on features customers care about.
- **0:** No comparison; visitor must do their own research.

### 32. A viral product is more expensive than its competitors
Nobody talks about the second-cheapest option. Charge more.
- **3:** Priced at or above competitors with confidence.
- **0:** Racing to the bottom on price as the main pitch.
